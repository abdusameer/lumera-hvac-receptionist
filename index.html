Salon · Book an appointment

Proposed configuration

  1. Service
  2. Preferred date
  3. Staff preference
  4. Available times
  5. Appointment summary

The receptionist matches the request to the salon's service list, so the appointment length comes from the service instead of a fixed slot.

Conversation

  1. Caller

    Hi, I'd like to book a gel manicure on Friday afternoon.

  2. Receptionist

    Happy to help. A gel manicure takes about 60 minutes. Is that this Friday?

  3. Caller

    Yes, this Friday, any time after noon.

  4. Receptionist

    Do you have a preferred nail technician, or is anyone available fine?

  5. Caller

    Maya, if she's free.

  6. Receptionist

    Maya has two 60-minute openings on Friday afternoon: 1:30 PM or 3:45 PM. Which works better?

  7. Caller

    3:45 PM, please. My name is Riley.

  8. Receptionist

    Thanks, Riley. I have a gel manicure with Maya on Friday at 3:45 PM.

Proposed configuration

  2. Location
  3. Urgency

  5. Caller

    88 Sample Lane, Glendale.

  6. Receptionist

Restaurant · Place a pickup order

Proposed configuration

  1. Menu
  2. Items and quantities
  3. Allowed changes
  4. Read-back

The receptionist takes items only from the menu it's given, accepts only the changes that menu allows and reads the order back before passing it on.

The same six steps

Every business, one dependable pattern.

  1. 01

    Answer the call.

    Every caller gets a consistent greeting, at any hour the line is configured to answer.

  2. 02

    Understand the request.

    The receptionist works out whether the caller wants an appointment, a repair, a table or an order.

  3. 03

    Collect the required details.

    It asks only the questions your business needs for that request.

  4. 04

    Check the connected system when available.

    Where a calendar or booking tool is connected, it checks real availability before offering options.

  5. 05

    Confirm the next step.

    The caller hears exactly what was recorded and what happens next.

  6. 06

    Pass organized information to the team.

    Your staff get a clear summary instead of a voicemail to decode.

Configured for you

What changes for each business.

The pattern stays the same. These parts are set up around how your team already works.

Services
What you offer, how long each takes and which staff can do it.
Salon: a gel removal and new set takes longer than a classic manicure.
Scheduling rules
Hours, buffers, service areas and how far ahead people can book.
HVAC: 60-minute diagnostic visits, offered only in open calendar time.
Questions
The details your team needs before a visit or a call back.
Plumbing: is the leak active, and has the water been shut off?
Reservation capacity
Table sizes, seating length and how many guests can arrive at once.
Restaurant: a full 7:00 PM means offering 6:30 or 8:15 instead.
Menu options
Items, prices and the changes your kitchen accepts.
Restaurant: dressing on the side is fine; unlisted add-ons are declined.
Escalation procedures
When a person should take over, and how they're reached.
Any business: urgent or unusual calls go to your staff under rules you approve.
How it connectsTechnical view

The HVAC example is based on a workflow Lumera is developing. It receives requests from a voice agent and sends each one down one of three paths.

Request path in the HVAC workflow

  1. Voice agent
  2. Webhook
  3. Request abstraction
  4. Tool routing
  • Check availability Reads existing Google Calendar events and returns open times.
  • Book appointment Creates the calendar event and sends an email confirmation.
  • Qualify lead Captures service type, problem and urgency for follow-up.

Used in the HVAC workflow in development

Voice agent, webhooks, Google Calendar, email. Times in America/Los_Angeles, ISO 8601 dates, about 60-minute appointments.

Simulated on this page

Every conversation, opening, capacity check, menu and summary. The page connects to nothing and creates, sends or orders nothing.

Proposed, not built yet

Salon, plumbing and restaurant configurations. Reservation, ordering, payment, POS and staff-transfer integrations are chosen per business and named once they're verified.
